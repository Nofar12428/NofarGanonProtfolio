The challenge

Make learning part of a continuous game story.

Question-and-answer drills can feel tiring and leave little room for choice. We kept learning playful, letting players explore and choose while advancing a shared mission: repairing the spaceship.

The audience

Children in grades 2–4.

With varying levels of familiarity with digital games.

KEY DECISIONS

Four significant decisions.

01UX · Interaction design

Keep the collected part in the player’s hands

A warehouse and backpack added a step and made the part hard to track.

The part now stays in the astronaut’s hands until it is delivered or returned to space.

02UI · Visual feedback

Show where and when a part can be delivered

A flashing arrow showed the direction, but not where or when to deliver a part.

Collecting a part opens the spaceship’s legs to mark the return point, and a white light shows when delivery is possible.

03UX · Player onboarding

Adapt guidance to familiarity with the game

Testing showed that step-by-step on-screen hints didn’t explain the full sequence.

We added a full tutorial for new players, with a skip option and short tips for returning ones.

04UX · Navigation

Let players jump across space

Peer feedback highlighted that searching one end of space, finding nothing and scrolling all the way back was tiring.

Reaching either edge of space now sends the astronaut straight to the other side.
