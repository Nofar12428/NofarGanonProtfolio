ABOUT THE PROJECT

The product

A prototype that helps managers prepare for feedback conversations.

Tools
Lovable · Product prototype
Team
5 students
Duration
2.5 months
Context
Academic project · Stratasys

The challenge

Make training useful between the workshops.

Training happens twice a year, but feedback conversations happen all year. Managers needed practical guidance in the moment.

The audience

Stratasys managers worldwide.

With varying levels of experience in conducting feedback conversations.

AI & ethics

The tool supports preparation, and responsibility stays with the manager.
Guidance should rely on approved content and make AI’s limits clear.

How it works
01

Welcome

Three ways to start: an open chat, a ready scenario or a guided preparation.

02

Chat assistant

Describe the situation in your own words and get guidance for the conversation.

03

Feedback scenarios

Pick a common situation and open a conversation from it.

04

Quick preparation

Four short steps — type, goal, sensitivity and situation — then structured guidance.

KEY DECISIONS

Three significant decisions.

01UX · Content structure

Make guidance easy to scan before a conversation

Managers need guidance shortly before a conversation, not lengthy learning materials.

Every path uses the same structure: an opening, a key message, talking points and a common pitfall.

02AI · Transparency

Be transparent about the prototype’s capabilities

Some responses need an AI engine that isn’t connected yet.

We clearly labeled prepared content and future capabilities, so no one assumes every answer is live.

03Content · Product language

Use language that fits an internal tool

The tool had to feel familiar to managers at work.

We named it Feedback Copilot and kept the copy short and direct, without emojis or marketing language.

My contribution.

Worked with a team to define the product and build its prototype in Lovable.

  • Product definition. Helped turn client needs into user flows, content structure and guidance.
  • Product boundaries. Helped define the tool’s role and what it should and shouldn’t do.
  • Review and refinement. Reviewed the content, interface and design against the specification.

What’s next?

To move from prototype to an organizational tool:

  • Test with managers. Observe real preparation to see where the guidance helps and where it falls short.
  • Refine the product. Update content and flows based on testing, reviewed by organizational professionals.
  • Start with a pilot. Gather feedback from a small rollout before expanding access.
